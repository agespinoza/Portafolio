Mining engineering × data science Cerro Alto, a fictional porphyry copper mine

From drillhole to copper.

One fictional open-pit mine followed end to end: drilling and assay QA/QC, a geostatistical resource model, pit optimisation, a year of haul-fleet and plant data, a recovery model and the monthly reconciliation that tells the mine whether its model can be trusted.

                              11 Method

                              How it was built

                              Geology and estimation

                              A synthetic deposit with a leached cap, a supergene blanket and hypogene ore was drilled with 88 holes in four campaigns. Assays were composited to bench height, top-cut at the 99th percentile by domain and estimated by ordinary block kriging inside hard domain boundaries.

                              Mine planning

                              Eleven nested pit shells were solved exactly as a maximum-closure problem with a 1:5 slope pattern. A starter pit and two pushbacks are scheduled month by month so that every block's precedence is mined first; an automated test checks it.

                              Operations and data science

                              A year of truck time records, daily plant data and grade-control sampling was simulated from the same blocks. The recovery model is a ridge regression trained on the first nine months and tested on the last three, chosen by expanding-window cross-validation.

                              Honest about the data

                              Every number is fictional. Because the true grade is known everywhere, the console can show what a real mine never sees: how much the kriged model smooths the truth. This is not a public report under JORC, NI 43-101 or S-K 1300.
