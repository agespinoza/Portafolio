Model monitoring Cattle TB screening on public data

When the herds change, does the model?

A screening model flags cattle herds for a confirmatory tuberculosis test. The share of infected herds it sees changes over the years. This console replays real public statistics from Great Britain and Ireland, scales the herds screened by US cattle imports from Mexico, and shows when a fixed threshold goes stale, how a team can notice without waiting for lab results, and what each response costs.

Simulation on public data From 2015 to 2016 I was IT Manager at the Comité Estatal para el Fomento y Protección Pecuaria de Baja California (CEFPPBC). Our team managed the farm and cattle-shipment records used in bovine tuberculosis analysis for cattle sent to the United States: I scanned those records and loaded them into databases that we then used for analysis. This demo uses none of that data: it runs on simulated herds driven by public international statistics.

          What this shows, and what it does not

          The simulated model

          Each quarter a number of herds is scored. A clean herd scores around 0 and an infected one around 2, with the same spread, so the test is useful but imperfect. The model flags a herd when its score is above a threshold chosen to minimize cost for the share of infected herds it expects. Missing an infected herd costs more than a false alarm, as set in the controls.

          The drift

          Only the share of infected herds changes, following the real series for the chosen region (label shift). Great Britain reports herds that are not officially TB-free at quarter end; Ireland reports herds restricted at quarter end. The definitions are similar but not identical, so the levels should not be compared like for like.

          Limits

          The data are national or regional totals, so nothing here describes a farm, a shipment, or a cause. Cattle imports from Mexico only scale how many herds are scored; they are not infection data. Months with no cattle imports have no row in the source and count as zero. No data from any employer is used.
